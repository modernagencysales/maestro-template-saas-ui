# App Idea Evaluator Language Bank

Status: captured verbatim for product discovery and future messaging work.

This document preserves the strongest language from the product conversation
before positioning, UX copy, or implementation work edits it. Quoted language
should remain verbatim unless a later decision explicitly replaces it.

## Core Promise

> Tell me if my product is good.

> Tell me if my idea is good.

> Know what it will take.

> Find out what it will really take to build your app in 10 minutes.

The core customer progression is:

> Tell me whether my idea is good, help me make it better, and let me know what
> it will take to build it.

## Customer And Context

> This is a low ticket product for people who have an idea but don't really know
> what to do to get it done.

> They are nontechnical.

> They would probably go to Lovable and get locked into their infra but I want
> them to buy my template instead.

> It gives you a spec you can immediately take to a dev agency or to a coding
> agent and have a lot more clarity.

> I think it will probably end up several hundred dollars but for now I will try
> it cheap.

## Product Shape

> It should be kind of a free thing that almost roasts the user a little bit and
> tells them if the product is good.

> If it isn't good, it suggests how it could be better.

> If it is good, it gives them a free report and tells them what's exclusive.

> Maybe it's more like a freebie and then on the thank you page of getting the
> freebie, you can get the whole template.

## Primary Positioning

> Tell me your app idea. I'll tell you whether it's worth building, roast what's
> weak, and show you how to make it stronger.

> Know what to build before spending weeks or thousands.

> Should I Build This?

Primary action:

> Roast my app idea

Core product boundary:

> Free: Should I build this?

> Paid: Exactly how should I build it?

The free product creates the decision. The paid product creates execution
readiness.

## Voice And Roast Language

The intended personality is a sharp, experienced friend who wants the user to
succeed, rather than a comedian trying to humiliate them.

The roast follows a constructive sequence:

1. Say the uncomfortable thing.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Show how to improve it.
4. Give the user a concrete next move.

Preserve these example lines:

> You've described a feature, not a business. That's fixable.

> Your target customer is "everyone," which usually means you haven't chosen one
> yet.

> The problem sounds real. Your solution currently sounds like ChatGPT wearing a
> different hat.

> This is crowded, but your niche gives you a believable way in.

> You've built a feature looking for a founder.

> Good news: people have this problem. Bad news: they already solve it with a
> spreadsheet and spite.

## Verdict Language

- Worth testing
- Promising, but blurry
- Strong problem, weak solution
- Good product, unclear distribution
- Feature rather than business
- Crowded, but winnable
- Too expensive for version one
- Needs a different customer
- Do not build this yet
- Expensive science project

## Free Report Language

The free experience should answer:

> Can this app be built?

It should provide:

- the verdict and score;
- the sharpest one-paragraph roast;
- the strongest part of the idea;
- the biggest weakness;
- a rewritten, stronger version of the concept;
- the ideal first customer;
- the smallest version worth testing;
- a seven-day validation experiment;
- three questions that must be answered before building;
- a buildability score;
- the simplest useful first version;
- essential screens and the primary workflow;
- likely complexity and cost;
- the three biggest traps;
- the most appropriate implementation path.

Do not blur or withhold the verdict. The honesty is what earns trust.

## Paid Transition Language

> Your idea is clearer. Now turn it into something a developer or coding agent
> can actually build.

The paid deliverable is the Complete Build Pack. It may include:

- complete product specification;
- screen-by-screen requirements;
- user journeys;
- data model;
- workflows and business rules;
- acceptance criteria;
- integrations;
- edge cases and failure states;
- phased implementation plan;
- agency briefing;
- coding-agent kickoff prompt;
- personalized Maestro implementation path.

Recommended funnel language:

> Free Buildability Report -> Complete Build Pack -> Maestro build system ->
> optional implementation help

Results-page actions:

- Keep my free app map
- Get the complete developer-ready Build Pack
- Start building this with Maestro

## Shareable Result

> Your app idea scored 72/100
>
> Verdict: Good product, unclear distribution
>
> Brutal truth: You know what to build, but not how customers will find it.

## Trust Principle

Only recommend Maestro when it genuinely fits. If the idea is poor, help the
user improve it and rerun the evaluation. If every result ends with "buy my
template," the honesty collapses.

The template should be presented as the implementation path, not merely a
download:

> Start building my app from this plan.

The eventual offer can combine the template, personalized project generation,
and a guided launch workflow.

## Language To Avoid In Primary Marketing

Do not lead with:

- specification;
- MCP;
- architecture;
- infrastructure lock-in.

Prefer:

- app plan;
- build roadmap;
- developer-ready brief;
- know what it will take;
- know what to build before spending weeks or thousands.
